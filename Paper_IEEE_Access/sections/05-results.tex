\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
Unless stated otherwise, every table reports current-protocol numbers: \texttt{center\_mixed} training with 80\% \texttt{center\_shift} and 20\% \texttt{center\_zoom}, 150 epochs, model selection by image-level merged validation Dice under \texttt{center\_shift}, and image-level merged test evaluation. The two test prompt conditions, covering (\texttt{center\_zoom}) and off-center (\texttt{center\_shift}), are always reported separately. The ablation study in Section~\ref{sec:ablation} is the one exception and is still being refreshed under repeated seeds.

Higher resolution helps at every step. Dice follows $512>256>128$ on both datasets and under both prompt conditions, and FracAtlas gains more, which fits its thin fracture lesions losing proportionally more detail when the image shrinks. Normalized, boundary error stays near $0.03$ across the BTXRD resolutions and actually falls on FracAtlas, from about $0.044$ to $0.015$, so it does not worsen as the frame grows. Each model is evaluated only on its own dataset; no cross-dataset claim is made.

\subsection{Comparison Against Automatic and Prompt-Matched Attention U-Net}
\label{sec:baseline}
Left to find the lesion on its own, Attention U-Net manages a Dice of only 0.517 on BTXRD and 0.342 on FracAtlas at $512\times512$, with HD95 past 120 pixels on both (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). That number is not an architecture verdict, since this model never sees the box; it is a measure of how hard automatic localization is, which is exactly the step the prompt removes.

Give the same box to a plain Attention U-Net and the picture changes but a gap remains. On BTXRD, PGA-UNet is ahead of the prompt-channel variant by 0.022 Dice under covering prompts and 0.026 under off-center prompts, and ahead of the prompt-crop variant by about 0.041 in both. On FracAtlas those gaps grow to 0.134 / 0.125 and 0.137 / 0.151. HD95 is lower for PGA-UNet than for either variant on both datasets.

\subsection{Attention U-Net Top-Dice and Bottom-Dice Subsets}
\label{sec:extreme}
Where does that margin come from? We ranked the test images by Attention U-Net Dice, kept the top 50 and the bottom 50 per dataset, and re-ran the prompt-guided models on those exact stems (Table~\ref{tab:extreme}). On the top-50, where Attention U-Net is already at 0.872 on BTXRD and 0.493 on FracAtlas, the prompt-guided models bunch together. On the bottom-50, Attention U-Net falls apart, to 0.031 and 0.169, while PGA-UNet still holds 0.712 and 0.687 under covering prompts. The two conventional prompt baselines recover too, but less: PGA-UNet leads them on the BTXRD bottom-50 by 0.044 to 0.074 Dice and on the FracAtlas bottom-50 by 0.104 to 0.189. These groups are defined by Attention U-Net, not by any objective notion of difficulty, so the only claim is that the benefit of prompt-conditioned processing is concentrated exactly where automatic localization breaks down.

\subsection{Matched Comparison Against SAM-Med2D}
\label{sec:sam}
Both models get the same box at $256\times256$ (Table~\ref{tab:sam}). Zero-shot, SAM-Med2D barely works on bone radiographs: 0.255 Dice on BTXRD, 0.262 on FracAtlas. Fine-tuning on the same split and prompt protocol lifts it to 0.630 and 0.563, but PGA-UNet at that resolution is at 0.762 and 0.629 under covering prompts, ahead by 0.132 and 0.066 Dice and by a similar amount in CBL. SAM-Med2D pulls ahead in only one place, boundary distance on FracAtlas, where its fine-tuned HD95 of 8.0 pixels beats PGA-UNet's 10.6. Absolute accuracy and the covering-to-off-center change are separate questions; Section~\ref{sec:robust} takes up the second.

\subsection{Performance on Small Lesions}
\label{sec:small}
If keeping weak evidence alive through the network matters anywhere, it is here. The small-lesion subset is the 50 test images per dataset with the least total lesion area, 7 to 433 pixels on BTXRD and 145 to 944 on FracAtlas. Table~\ref{tab:small} scores three comparisons on it in a shared $512\times512$ frame.

SAM-Med2D struggles badly at this size: fine-tuned, it reaches only 0.256 Dice on BTXRD and 0.371 on FracAtlas, and zero-shot it is close to nothing, while PGA-UNet at $256$ holds 0.705 and 0.648. The conventional prompt baselines at $512$ trail PGA-UNet by 0.04 to 0.11 Dice on BTXRD and 0.11 to 0.16 on FracAtlas, and the prompt-free Attention U-Net drops to around 0.28. Resolution bites hardest here too: on the fixed small-lesion stems, PGA-UNet Dice falls from 0.766 at $512$ to 0.714 at $256$ and 0.631 at $128$ on BTXRD, and from 0.720 to 0.642 and 0.609 on FracAtlas, a $512$-to-$128$ loss of 0.135 and 0.112, more than on the full test set.

\subsection{Sensitivity to Prompt Displacement}
\label{sec:robust}
Moving the box off-center costs PGA-UNet very little at $512\times512$: 0.008 Dice on BTXRD, 0.014 on FracAtlas, with CBL down by 0.006 and 0.015. On the small-lesion subset the loss stays under 0.01 Dice on both datasets. The conventional prompt baselines lose a comparable or slightly larger amount, and fine-tuned SAM-Med2D about 0.03. All of this is sensitivity to one displacement rule fixed in advance. It says nothing about arbitrary radiologist-drawn boxes, and partial or off-target boxes were never tested.

\subsection{Monte Carlo Cross-Validation}
\label{sec:mccv}
One fixed split could flatter or hurt the method by chance, so PGA-UNet at $512\times512$ was retrained on four fresh random image-level splits, with the training seed held at 22120196 and only the split seed changed (Table~\ref{tab:mccv}). Dice varies by at most 0.011 across splits on either dataset and either prompt condition; CBL by at most 0.013. The FracAtlas repeated-split mean, 0.733, sits just above the fixed-split test result of 0.727, so if anything the reported split is slightly harder than typical. This is evidence that PGA-UNet is stable across splits, not that it beats any baseline.

\subsection{Efficiency}
\label{sec:efficiency}
PGA-UNet is small: 2.955M parameters, all trainable, an 11.4 MB checkpoint, 7.74 GFLOPs at $256\times256$ and 31.0 at $512\times512$. On the Tesla T4 it runs in 8.5 ms per image at $256$ and 18.5 ms at $512$, and on CPU in 112 ms and 375 ms. Fine-tuned SAM-Med2D at $256\times256$ carries 271.24M parameters, a 2.4 GB checkpoint, and 92.0 GFLOPs, and takes 50.1 ms on GPU and 1324 ms on CPU. At the matched resolution PGA-UNet is therefore about 92 times lighter in parameters, 12 times lighter in FLOPs, 215 times smaller on disk, and 6 times faster on GPU. These are measured costs on one implementation, one GPU, and two resolutions, nothing more.

\subsection{Exploratory Comparison of Segmentation Losses}
\label{sec:loss}
Because the targets are small, we also tried swapping the Dice term for a size-conditioned Tversky loss, and separately for a Focal Dice loss, everything else held fixed. Neither helped. On BTXRD the default Dice + BCE gives 0.782 / 0.774 Dice (covering / off-center), against 0.778 / 0.767 for Tversky and 0.773 / 0.765 for Focal Dice. On FracAtlas the default gives 0.727 / 0.713, against 0.709 / 0.680 for Tversky and 0.642 / 0.578 for Focal Dice, the last a plain regression with HD95 above 100 pixels. We keep this as a negative control: under the current protocol the default objective is already a good choice, and neither alternative is adopted.

\subsection{Failure Modes}
\label{sec:failure}
PGA-UNet still fails, and the failures have a pattern. In the qualitative review, low Dice clusters on lesions that are elongated or branching, that sit in cluttered anatomy such as the shoulder or clavicle, or that barely stand out from the bone around them. A covering box does not fix a case where the local image evidence is itself poor.

\subsection{Training-Free Self-Assessment and Candidate Suggestion}
\label{sec:selfassess-results}
Without any ground truth, the score $Q$ from Section~\ref{sec:selfassess} still tracks the true per-prompt Dice. Its Spearman correlation with the true Dice is 0.70 (covering) and 0.60 (off-center) on BTXRD, and 0.65 and 0.48 on FracAtlas. The two cues pull their weight: mask decisiveness alone reaches a Spearman near 0.4, jitter stability near 0.5 to 0.6, and their mean is at least as good as either. We also trained a small head to regress the Dice directly; it collapsed to a near-constant output, Spearman under 0.2 on both datasets, and is not used.

Turned around, $Q$ can rank 50 random candidate boxes and keep the top five (Figure~\ref{fig:demo-suggest}). At least one of the five then covers more than half the lesion for 67\% of BTXRD images and 82\% of FracAtlas images, and the best of the five covers 0.84 and 0.90 of the lesion area on average; a top-three list gives 58\% / 78\% and 0.77 / 0.87. This is a review aid and nothing stronger: $Q$ is a heuristic, not a calibrated probability, and the shortlist is not a detector.
