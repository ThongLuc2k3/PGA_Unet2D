\begin{abstract}
Bone X-ray lesion segmentation remains challenging because lesions are often tiny, low-contrast, and visually entangled with surrounding anatomy. In these cases, the bottleneck is frequently localization rather than contour refinement. Practical pipelines often address this by narrowing the search region before segmentation. We take a direct interactive variant of that idea: a clinician supplies a coarse bounding box, and the model uses it as spatial guidance.

We propose PGA-UNet, a lightweight prompt-guided CNN that converts the box prompt into a Gaussian-smoothed plateau heatmap, amplifies prompt-relevant encoder features through a Prompt Spatial Gate (PSG), and preserves prompt influence in the decoder through a Conditional Attention Decoder (CAD). The aim is not only to indicate where segmentation should occur, but to sustain weak small-lesion evidence through the network.

Attention U-Net is used only as an automatic baseline to illustrate task difficulty without prompt guidance. Under covering prompts at $512\times512$, PGA-UNet achieved Dice scores of 0.8788 on BTXRD and 0.8286 on FracAtlas, compared with 0.5160 and 0.3075 for Attention U-Net. The prompt-matched comparison is with SAM-Med2D. At $256\times256$, PGA-UNet outperformed fine-tuned SAM-Med2D under both covering and off-center prompts on both datasets, although PGA-UNet was trained only on covering prompts while SAM-Med2D saw both. The largest margin appeared on the 50 smallest BTXRD lesions, where SAM-Med2D dropped to Dice 0.1903 while PGA-UNet reached 0.7650; center-based localization also favored PGA-UNet (CBL 0.9261 versus 0.4489), indicating a substantial reduction in spatial drift.

Ablation showed that PSG and CAD are most effective in combination, and that the Gaussian prompt is more beneficial than a binary box only within the full architecture. PGA-UNet uses 2.95M parameters, reducing parameter count, FLOPs, and GPU inference latency by approximately 92$\times$, 11.9$\times$, and 6.0$\times$ relative to SAM-Med2D in the tested $256\times256$ setting. These results suggest that, for very small lesions, prompts are most effective when treated as a dense spatial prior that remains active from early encoding through final decoding.
\end{abstract}
