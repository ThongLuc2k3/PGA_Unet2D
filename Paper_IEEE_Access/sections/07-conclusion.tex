\section{Conclusion}
This article presented PGA-UNet, a lightweight prompt-guided CNN for interactive bone X-ray lesion segmentation. The central design treats a user-supplied lesion-covering box as a dense spatial prior and reuses it through encoder-side PSG and decoder-side CAD. The manuscript is scoped as a controlled prompted-segmentation study: it compares automatic and prompt-matched baselines, evaluates controlled covering and off-center prompts, and examines small-lesion behavior, repeated image-level splits, ablations, and computational cost within that defined protocol. The conclusions are therefore limited to simulated lesion-covering boxes derived from annotations and do not establish unconstrained detection, performance for partial or negative boxes, radiologist-provided prompt behavior, calibrated confidence, patient-level generalization, or cross-dataset transfer. Future work should refresh all current-protocol quantitative tables after retraining, evaluate broader prompt conditions if the task scope is expanded, and validate the auxiliary QualityHead score before interpreting it as a calibrated probability.
